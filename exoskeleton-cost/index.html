Exoskeleton cost: what they cost and who pays

Exoskeletons now come in two very different kinds. Consumer ones for walking and hiking cost about the same as a good e-bike. Medical ones that help people with spinal cord injuries stand and walk cost as much as a house deposit, but health schemes can pay most of it. Here are the prices, the funding routes by country, and a calculator for what you would pay yourself.

Prices checked November 2026. Funding rules change and are often decided case by case, so check with your insurer, clinic or scheme before you commit.

What would you pay yourself?

Pick an example or type in your own quote, in dollars or pounds. The share your insurer or scheme pays is something you need to confirm with them.

With Medicare Part B you usually pay 20% of the approved amount for medical equipment after the yearly deductible (Medicare.gov). On a $91,032 exoskeleton, 20% is about $18,200, so ask whether a supplement plan, the VA or a charity can cover that share.

Questions to ask before you buy or apply

  1. Is there a trial? Schemes such as the NDIS may ask for a trial or rental first, and so should you.
  2. Who pays for training? Medical exoskeletons need sessions with a therapist before home use.
  3. What does servicing cost after the warranty? Ask for the price of batteries and yearly checks.
  4. Can it be rented? Renting first tells you whether you will use it every day.
  5. If cover is refused, can you appeal? Many approvals are case by case, and appeals do succeed.
