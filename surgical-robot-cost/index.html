Surgical robot cost: what a da Vinci costs a hospital

A surgical robot costs a hospital millions to buy, then hundreds of thousands a year to keep running, plus instruments for every operation. Here are the published figures from US filings and NHS records, how hospitals lease instead of buying, and a calculator for the cost per operation.

This page is about the cost to hospitals, not what a patient pays. Figures checked November 2026. Each one shows its source and date.

Buy, lease or pay per use?

  • Intuitive (da Vinci) leases many of its systems. In the last quarter of 2024, 222 systems were placed on operating leases, 140 of them on usage-based leases where the hospital pays more the more it operates (Intuitive results, Jan 2025). It does not publish lease prices.
  • CMR Surgical (Versius) uses a managed service model, where the hospital pays over a contract of perhaps five or six years instead of buying upfront (MedTech Dive, 2019).

Pay per use moves the risk to the maker: if the robot sits idle, the hospital pays less. Buying is cheaper only if the robot is kept busy. Our robot lease vs buy calculator compares the two with your cost of money, tax and resale value.
