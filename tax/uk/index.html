Full expensing, AIA and Made Smarter: buy, hire purchase or lease a robot in the UK?

UK companies can write off the full cost of a robot in the year they buy it, and many manufacturers can get up to £20,000 towards it. Both change which way of paying is cheapest. Here are the rules, with their gov.uk sources, and a worked example run through our calculator.

Rules checked November 2026, including changes announced in the Autumn Budget 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Your accountant will know your own position.

The rules in brief

RuleWhat it meansSource
Full expensingCompanies can deduct 100% of the cost of new main-rate plant and machinery in the year they buy it. It is permanent. It does not cover assets bought to lease out.HM Treasury, gov.uk
Annual Investment Allowance (AIA)Up to £1 million a year written off in full. Open to sole traders, partnerships of individuals, and companies.gov.uk
40% first-year allowanceFor spending from 1 January 2026, including by leasing firms and unincorporated businesses. Rising to 50% from 1 January 2027.HM Treasury, Oct 2026
Main poolAnything not written off up front is relieved at 14% a year (18% before April 2026), falling to 12% from April 2027.gov.uk
Corporation tax25% main rate. 19% on profits of £50,000 or less, with marginal relief up to £250,000.gov.uk
Hire purchaseYou can claim capital allowances on the robot, but not on the interest.gov.uk
LeasingYou cannot claim allowances on a leased robot (unless it is a long funding lease). Lease rentals are a business expense instead.gov.uk
Made Smarter Adoption50% match funding of £1,000 to £20,000 for SME manufacturers in England, with advice. Check your region's page.Made Smarter (South West)

Worked example: a £50,000 cobot

We ran our example cobot through the calculator: £50,000 price plus 25% integration, 2,000 hours a year for 5 years, money worth 10% a year. Figures are total cost in today's money, after tax relief and any grant.

Three things stand out:

  • With full expensing, hire purchase wins. You get the tax back in year one but pay for the robot over time.
  • A Made Smarter grant widens the gap a lot. Grants go to the owner, so they help buying and hire purchase, not leasing or pay per result.
  • Without full expensing, the lease edges ahead. That matters for sole traders who have used up their AIA, and for businesses that can only claim 40%.

Step by step: why the cheapest option can cost more in total

This is the part that surprises people. Here is the same £50,000 cobot, year by year, so you can see where the money goes. Each figure is cash out in that year. A minus sign is money coming back: the resale value in year 5, or tax relief.

Before tax

Way of payingDay oneYear 1Year 2Year 3Year 4Year 5Total cashCost in today's money
Buy with cash£62,500£6,000£6,000£6,000£6,000−£4,000£82,500£80,060
Hire purchase£17,750£16,820£16,820£16,820£16,820£6,920£91,952£78,238
Lease£13,980£17,760£17,760£17,760£17,760£6,781£91,801£77,520

Buying has the smallest total, £82,500, yet the lease costs least in today's money. The reason is timing. Buying takes £62,500 on day one. The lease takes £13,980 and spreads the rest over five years. Both end with you owning the robot (the example lease has a £1 buy-out), so resale value is the same either way.

The extra £9,300 or so that the lease adds up to is its interest and fees, which work out at about 7.2% a year. The example assumes your money is worth 10% a year. Borrowing at 7.2% when your own money is worth 10% is a good deal, so the lease wins. Set your cost of capital below about 7% and buying with cash wins instead. Hire purchase is the same idea at its 7.5% rate.

Full expensing gives an owner 25% of the cost back in the first year. Buying and hire purchase both get it, because you own the robot from the start. The lease gets relief only as each payment is made. So hire purchase now has the best of both: tax back early, cash out late. That is why it moves from second to first.

Notice the resale in year 5 is smaller after tax: £10,000 comes back, but a quarter of it is taxed as a balancing charge, because the robot was written off in full.

VAT

If your business is VAT-registered, you normally reclaim the VAT, so it does not change which option is cheapest. It does change cash flow. On a purchase or hire purchase, VAT on the full price is due at the start, because hire purchase counts as a supply of goods when you take the robot (HMRC manual VATTOS9250). On a lease, VAT is added to each payment. If you are not VAT-registered, or you are buying for your home, VAT is a real cost: add 20% to the price and to every payment.

The year-by-year figures come straight from our calculator, using the example inputs on the method page. Tax is simplified: relief is counted in the year the cost falls, not when the tax is paid, and the calculator assumes the company has enough profit to use it.

Things that catch people out

  1. Full expensing is for companies only. Sole traders and partnerships use the AIA, which also gives 100% up to £1 million.
  2. Selling a fully written-off robot is taxed. The sale price comes back as a balancing charge. The calculator counts this.
  3. Made Smarter money is limited and regional. Apply before you order, not after.
  4. The pool rate drops again in April 2027. Anything you do not write off up front is relieved more slowly.
