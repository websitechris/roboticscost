Section 179 and bonus depreciation: buy, finance or lease a robot in the US?

US tax rules for 2026 let a business write off the full cost of a robot in the year it buys it. That changes which way of paying is cheapest. Here are the 2026 rules, with their IRS sources, and a worked example run through our calculator.

Rules checked November 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Your accountant will know your own position, including state taxes.

The 2026 rules in brief

Rule2026 figureSource
Section 179 limitUp to $2,560,000 of equipment can be expensed. The limit shrinks dollar for dollar once you place more than $4,090,000 of equipment in service in the year.IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32
Bonus depreciation100%, made permanent, for eligible property acquired after 19 January 2025.IRS, Jan 2026
Corporate tax rate21% federal. Pass-through businesses pay their owners' rates instead.26 U.S.C. §11(b)
Lease paymentsA true lease is rent: you deduct payments as you make them, and you cannot claim Section 179.IRS FAQ
$1 buyout leasesAn option to buy at a nominal price is a sign of a conditional sale. The IRS treats that as a purchase, so the write-off can apply.IRS FAQ, IRS Pub 946

Worked example: a $50,000 cobot

We ran our example cobot through the calculator: $50,000 price, 2,000 hours a year for 5 years, money worth 10% a year. "Finance" means a loan or $1 buyout lease, where you own the robot and can write it off (the calculator calls this hire purchase). Figures are total cost in today's money, after tax relief.

Way of payingNo tax relief21% tax, full write-off in year one21% tax, write-off spread over 5 years
Buy with cash$80,060$64,656$66,637
Finance ($1 buyout)$78,238$61,222 (cheapest)$63,203
Lease$77,520 (cheapest)$62,144$62,541 (cheapest)
Pay per result (RaaS)$93,071$74,416$74,416

Before tax, the lease is cheapest. With the 2026 full write-off, financing the robot and writing it off in year one wins, because you get the tax back early while paying for the robot over time. Spread the write-off over five years and the lease wins again. The gaps are small, so your own quotes decide it.

Things that catch people out

  1. Pay per result gets no write-off. You deduct the fees as you pay them, like rent, so tax does not change the order much.
  2. Section 179 cannot create a loss. It is limited to your business income for the year. Bonus depreciation has no such limit.
  3. Selling the robot later is taxed. If you wrote it off fully, what you sell it for is taxable income. The calculator counts this.
  4. Read the lease. Whether it is a true lease or a purchase depends on its terms, not its name.
