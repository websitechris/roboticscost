Instant asset write-off: buy, finance or lease a robot in Australia?

The $20,000 instant asset write-off is now permanent for small businesses. Most robots cost more than that, though, and above the limit the tax relief is much slower. That changes which way of paying is cheapest. Here are the rules, with their ATO sources, and two worked examples from our calculator.

Rules checked November 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Your accountant will know your own position.

The rules in brief

RuleWhat it meansSource
Instant asset write-offBusinesses with turnover under $10 million can deduct the full cost of each asset under $20,000. Permanent from 1 July 2026, and now law.ATO
Small business poolAssets of $20,000 or more go into a pool, written off at 15% in the first year and 30% a year after.ATO
Company tax25% for base rate entities, 30% otherwise.ATO
Lease paymentsLease expenses are a deductible operating expense.ATO

Worked example 2: a $50,000 cobot

Above $20,000 the robot goes into the pool at 15% in year one. Our calculator spreads the rest evenly over the years, which is close to, but not exactly, the pool's 30% a year. Treat these as a guide.

Way of payingCompany at 25%Company at 30%
Buy with cash$64,227$61,061
Finance$60,487$56,937
Lease$59,717 (cheapest)$56,156 (cheapest)
Pay per result (RaaS)$70,863$66,422

Above the write-off limit, the lease wins, because lease payments are deducted as you go while the pool relieves a bought robot slowly. The gap is small, so your own quotes decide it.

Things that catch people out

  1. The $20,000 limit is per asset. Ask your accountant whether installation and tooling count towards it.
  2. Turnover must be under $10 million to use the write-off and the pool.
  3. Selling a written-off robot is taxed. The sale price comes back as income.
  4. Check how your finance is set up. Whether you count as the owner, and can claim the write-off, depends on the contract. Ask before you sign.
