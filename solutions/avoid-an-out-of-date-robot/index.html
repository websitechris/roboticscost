How to avoid being stuck with an out-of-date robot

AI is moving fast. A robot you buy today may look dated in two or three years, so owning it for five can make little sense. Here are five ways to keep the robot's work without being stuck with old kit, with real examples and their sources.

Checked November 2026. Few makers publish their upgrade or buy-back terms, so treat every example as a starting point and get the terms in writing. Where we couldn't confirm something, we say so.

Buying for your home? If you'll want the newest model anyway, subscribing or leasing saves the hassle of selling the old one. The rest of this page is mainly for businesses.

First: will your robot actually date?

Not every robot ages at the same speed, so check this before you pay extra for flexibility.

Type of robotHow fast it datesWhy
Industrial arms, cobots, floor-cleaning robotsSlowlyThe mechanics change little and the software is often updated. There's a real used market: KUKA sells overhauled robots up to 18 years old (KUKA).
Humanoids, home robots, AI-heavy machinesFastNew models arrive every year or two, and the AI is most of what you're paying for. Expect a low resale value.

To see what dating does to your own sums, use the calculator's fast-changing tech setting.

1. Lease with an upgrade option

Some robot leases let you swap to newer kit during or at the end of the term.

  • Universal Robots (cobots), with lender DLL: customers can "upgrade to new equipment, or add cobots anytime during the contract term". At the end they can buy the cobot, "upgrade to newer technology, extend the finance term or simply return the equipment" (MMH, date not shown).
  • Intuitive (da Vinci surgical robots): offers leases of up to 84 months and "technology obsolescence protection", though not in every country (Intuitive booklet, June 2024).
  • Omron (industrial robots, UK): "Once the lease is over, you are free to upgrade" (Omron, April 2023).

Watch for: an upgrade usually means a new lease. Ask whether the term restarts, how much of what you've paid counts towards the new robot, and what an early swap costs. None of the sources above publish those figures.

2. Pay per result (robotics as a service)

You pay for the robot's work, and the provider owns the robot. When the technology moves on, upgrading is their problem.

  • Formic (US factories): industrial robots at "a low hourly rate with guaranteed uptime and no upfront costs". Formic buys and maintains the robots (Robotics 24/7, Aug 2021). No hourly price published.
  • Seegrid (warehouse robots): a subscription for a set term, which customers can continue "by renewing or upgrading" (FreightWaves, June 2021).
  • Intuitive: a pay-per-use plan for surgical robots. "Don't use = don't pay" (Intuitive booklet, June 2024).
  • 1X NEO (home humanoid): $20,000 to buy or $499 a month (1X, April 2026). See our rent vs buy break-even for NEO.

Watch for: a contract that keeps you on the same model for its whole term. Ask in writing whether newer models are included and when. More on how RaaS is priced.

3. Maker buy-back and trade-in

Like part-exchanging a car, the maker takes the old robot towards a new one.

  • KUKA: will "evaluate the robot for potential repurchase", and offers a "part-exchange" when you buy a new robot (KUKA).
  • ABB: owners can "sell inactive or legacy robots to ABB" through a buy-back service (MMH, date not shown).
  • Intuitive: when da Vinci 5 launched, buyers of the older Xi could secure rights to the new model "with a deferral of a portion of the purchase price" (MassDevice, March 2024).

Watch for: as with cars, the maker sets the trade-in price, and it's rarely generous. Get a guaranteed figure, or a formula, before you buy. Then compare it with selling the robot yourself. We found no maker that publishes trade-in values for robots.

4. Modular upgrades: keep the machine, change the brain

A well-built robot body can outlast its electronics and software. If parts can be swapped, you upgrade the part instead of the whole robot.

  • Tools and grippers. Cobot and industrial arms take interchangeable tools, so a new job may only need a new gripper.
  • Add-on modules. Boston Dynamics sells Spot's arm as a "payload", an add-on for the robot (Boston Dynamics). We couldn't confirm the price or whether it fits every older Spot.
  • Upgrades to existing robots. The Universal Robots lease above lets customers "upgrade existing cobots".
  • Software updates. Many newer robots get software updates over the internet. Ask how many years of updates are included, and what they cost after that.
  • Compute modules. Swapping the robot's computer for a newer one would be the biggest win for AI-heavy robots. We haven't yet found a maker that publishes a price for this, so ask.

5. Buy refurbished, or sell second-hand

If a robot dates slowly, buying a refurbished one cuts the cost of the risk. And a working used market means your own robot is worth something at the end.

  • ABB remanufactured robots: each goes through a "152-point inspection", is tested under load for at least 16 hours, and comes with a 24-month warranty (ABB).
  • KUKA used robots: three grades, from ex-display robots 1 to 3 years old with a full warranty, to overhauled robots 8 to 18 years old. KUKA also rents for a minimum of 3 months, with the rent "proportionately deducted from the purchase price" if you buy (KUKA).
  • Intuitive: rentals of up to 24 months, with payments credited towards a later purchase or lease (Intuitive booklet, June 2024).
  • Used marketplaces: sites such as Machineseeker list more than a thousand used industrial robots.

Home and humanoid robots have very little second-hand market so far, so don't count on selling one for much.

Which route fits you

Your situationBest route to look at first
Fast-changing robot, uncertain usePay per result, or a short rental
Fast-changing robot, steady useA lease with an upgrade option
Slow-changing robot, heavy useBuy (new or refurbished) and agree a buy-back or trade-in up front
Good machine, ageing software or toolsModular upgrades before replacing the whole robot
Home robot, you like the latest modelSubscribe or lease

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Can I upgrade to a newer model during the contract, and what does it cost?
  2. Will you guarantee a buy-back or trade-in value, or a formula for one, in writing?
  3. How many years of software updates are included?
  4. Which parts can be swapped later: tools, arm, battery, computer?
  5. How long will you supply spare parts for this model?
  6. If I lease, does an upgrade restart the term?
