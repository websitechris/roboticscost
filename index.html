What will a robot really cost you?

Buy it, finance it, lease it, or pay per result? Sellers each push the option that suits them. This site does the sums neutrally, in today's money, including the costs that quotes leave out.

Compare buy, hire purchase, lease and pay per result

Four ways to pay for a robot

OptionYou payUsually suits
BuyThe price, integration and running costs. You keep any resale value.Heavy, steady use over several years, when you can fund it cheaply
Hire purchaseA deposit, then monthly payments with interest. You own the robot at the end.Owning without paying it all up front, while keeping grants and tax write-downs
LeaseA monthly payment with interest built in, usually plus running costsSpreading the cost when cash is tight, if the rate is fair
Pay per resultA fee per month, hour or task. Maintenance is usually included.Trials, light or uncertain use, and fast-changing technology

Which one is cheapest depends mostly on how many hours the robot works, your cost of money, and what the robot is worth at the end. The calculator shows all four side by side and tells you what would change the answer.
