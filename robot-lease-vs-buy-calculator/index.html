How to read the result

The main figure is the cost in today's money, also called net present value. A payment in year five costs you less than the same payment today, because you keep the money working in the meantime. Your cost of capital sets how much less. Adding up the cash instead flatters any option that pushes payments later.

Cost per working hour spreads that figure evenly over the years and divides by the hours the robot actually works, after downtime. It is the fairest single number for comparing with a wage or a quote priced by the hour.

Hire purchase or lease?

Both spread the cost over monthly payments. The difference is ownership. With hire purchase you own the robot once the last payment is made, so you keep its resale value and can usually claim grants and the tax write-down for buying equipment. With a lease the provider owns it. Compare the two interest rates first, then what you get at the end.

Robot leasing: the questions to ask

A lease is a loan in a different form. The provider buys the robot and you repay it monthly, with interest built in. Quotes usually show the payment and leave out the rate. The calculator works the rate out for you, including fees and payment timing.

  • What is the interest rate? Compare it with what your bank would charge to lend you the same money.
  • What happens at the end? A nominal buy-out leaves the robot with you. Handing it back means the provider keeps its resale value, which is part of what you paid for.
  • Who pays for maintenance, insurance and integration? They are rarely in the monthly figure.
  • Can you end it early, and at what cost?

What sellers rarely mention

  • Hours of use decide most cases. Return-on-investment examples often assume two or three shifts. Try one shift and see what changes.
  • The robot is only part of the bill. Integration, tooling, guarding and training can add a large share again.
  • Resale value is uncertain. Robots lose value as newer models arrive. Test a low figure.
  • Pay-per-result contracts have small print. Check yearly price rises, minimum terms, usage caps and exit fees.
  • Flexibility has value the numbers can't show. Paying per result lets you walk away when the technology moves on. Decide what that is worth to you.
